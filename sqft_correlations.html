Observations

How to read what follows

This brief treats ZIP 77080 single-family sold price per square foot as a local tape, then sets it against the metro and county series that actually move the bid: jobs, inventory, mortgage rates, household income, business formation, net migration, and school quality.

77080 is not one neighborhood. It is the western Spring Branch belt: 1950s–70s ranch stock (Spring Shadows, Campbell Woods, Binglewood, Spring Branch Estates) sitting next to 2018–22 infill (Kolbe Farms, Neuen Manor, Pinecrest, Avondale Square). Redfin prints the ZIP at $204/sqft in September 2026. The broader HAR Spring Branch market area — which includes higher-priced 77055 and 77043 — printed $214.20 in 2024. Do not stack 77080 comps against Memorial-side Spring Branch.

The notes are a research readout, not a valuation model and not a buy or sell call. “Correlation” here means the overlays move together in a way you can see on the chart — not a claimed causal coefficient, and not a forecast of next year’s median. Neighborhood $/sqft is mix-dependent: a year heavy with new-build closings can lift the median even if ranch pricing is flat. Employment, income, establishments, and population are Harris County or Houston MSA prints. Use them as context, not as a substitute for block-level comps.

What follows is a six-point summary of the key observations, then the charts, then a longer discussion of each overlay, disclosures, and sources.

Summary of key observations

  1. 01 — A slower path than the Heights, and size still matters. The research composite runs from about $43.50/sqft in 1997 to $208 in 2025 — roughly 4.8×. The HAR-anchored stretch is cleaner: Spring Branch market-area median sold $/sqft rose from $80.92 in 2009 to $214.20 in 2024. Cottages (≤1,600 sqft) print a land-driven premium; large homes (≥2,600) trade at a discount. 2025–26 is a stall, not a new high: Redfin ZIP $/sqft is $204, down 7.3% year over year, while the median sale price is still ~$400k (+1.2%).
  2. 02 — Jobs and income still underwrite the bid. Houston MSA employment remains in a long expansion near 3.51 million. Harris County median household income reached $74,682 in 2024. 77080 captures a share of that job base through Memorial City, the Energy Corridor commute, and I-10 / Beltway 8 access — not through inner-loop scarcity.
  3. 03 — Inventory is not the amplifier it is inside the loop. 77080 months of supply has rebuilt toward ~5.8 — near a balanced ~6-month market, and looser than the Heights. New-build pockets (Neuen Manor, Pinecrest, Kolbe Farms) and 1950s lot grids both add supply the historic Heights does not. Redfin DOM is 60 days; sale-to-list is 97.6%; about 39% of listings have had a price drop.
  4. 04 — Mortgage rates remain the swing factor — and this ZIP feels them. The 2018 and 2022–23 hikes coincide with slower $/sqft gains. Sub-4% rates in 2020–21 lined up with the fastest recent run. At ~6.7%, payment-constrained demand is the working assumption. 77080’s buyer pool is more rate-sensitive than cash-heavy inner-loop pockets.
  5. 05 — Schools are a drag, not a premium. Cedar Brook Elementary is the local public anchor (GreatSchools 7/10, TEA B / 88). Spring Branch Elementary is solid at 6. Spring Woods High is mixed-to-weak (GreatSchools 3–4). Northbrook High is weak (2/10). Nearby private options exist, but they do not expand the buyer pool the way Field / Sinclair do in the Heights.
  6. 06 — County migration is mixed; 77080 is not scarce. Harris County still grows, with international inflows offsetting weaker domestic net. That is a metro absorption story. Inside 77080, the binding constraint is not lot scarcity — it is the mix of aging ranch stock and new-build supply, plus a weaker school print than the Memorial side of the same district.

Detail

Key observations, expanded

The summary above is the tape in six lines. The notes below unpack the same six points with the numbers, the lags, and the limits of each overlay.

01. The price path — and the size spread

HAR’s Spring Branch market-area median sold $/sqft printed $80.92 in 2009 and $214.20 in 2024. That is the cleanest public series for this geography. The 1997–2008 stretch is a research composite scaled into that 2009 print — treat it as directional, not an audited restatement. The 2025 ZIP composite is $208, sitting between realtor.com’s December 2025 ZIP print of $214/sqft and Redfin’s September 2026 ZIP print of $204/sqft (−7.3% year over year).

The recent split is the practical takeaway. Redfin’s median sale price in 77080 is still about $400k (+1.2% year over year) while $/sqft is down 7.3%. That is a mix and size story, not a clean price decline: larger new-build closings can lift the median sale price while $/sqft falls. Zillow’s ZIP ZHVI is $343,517 (−1.7% year over year) — a typical-home value, not a sold median.

Size is the mix that hides inside the median. Chart 01 splits the tape into three research bands: cottages ≤1,600 sqft, mid-size 1,600–2,600 sqft, and large ≥2,600 sqft. By 2025 the composite puts cottages near ~$245/sqft, mid-size at the $208 median, and large product near ~$183/sqft. Compare comps inside the same size band, not against the ZIP median alone.

The ZIP mix itself is the other bias. Spring Shadows (median year built 1970, appraisal ~$436k, avg ~2,245 sqft) is a different product from Neuen Manor (2019, appraisal ~$375k, ~1,820 sqft), Pinecrest (2022), or Kolbe Farms (2018, appraisal ~$505k). Campbell Woods (1957) and Binglewood (1959) print lower still. A year heavy with Pinecrest or Neuen Manor closings is not the same tape as a year of Spring Shadows ranches.

02. Employment

Houston–The Woodlands–Sugar Land MSA employment recovered from the 2020 air pocket and sits near 3.51 million. 77080 captures a share of that job base through Memorial City, the Energy Corridor commute, and I-10 / Beltway 8 access. Employment is a demand foundation, not a precise one-for-one predictor of 77080 $/sqft in any given year. The 2020 jobs dip is visible on the overlay; the price series barely paused. That is consistent with a still-expanding metro — not with a supply-constrained pocket. 77080 does not have the historic-district floor the Heights does.

03. Inventory

Months of supply in the 77080 composite compressed toward about 2.2 months in the 2021 frenzy and has rebuilt toward about 5.8 by 2025. That is near a conventional ~6-month balanced market, and it has often run looser than the Heights. Tightness is not the mechanism here the way it is inside the loop.

The recent prints agree on direction even when the levels differ. Redfin: 60 days on market, sale-to-list 97.6%, 38.6% of listings with a price drop, 91 homes sold in September (−25% year over year). Zillow: 184 homes for sale, 29 days to pending, median list $393,500. realtor.com (December 2025) called the ZIP a buyer’s market, with 261 active listings. Houston metro single-family inventory is about 5.5 months. 77080 is not tighter than that metro print.

New-build supply is the local amplifier in the other direction. Neuen Manor (2019), Kolbe Farms (2018), Pinecrest (2022), and Avondale Square (2021) are still delivering product onto a 1950s grid that already has more lots than the Heights. When metro inventory floods, 77080 does not have the floor those historic-district blocks do.

04. Mortgage rates

The 30-year Freddie Mac PMMS ran near 3.0% in 2021, spiked to about 6.8% in 2023, and has hovered around 6.65–6.7% in 2024–25. Dual-axis overlays show slower $/sqft growth around the 2018 and 2022–23 hikes, and the fastest recent run during the sub-4% window. Correlation is directional and lagged — rates do not print tomorrow’s median.

77080’s buyer pool is more rate-sensitive than cash and equity-rich buyers in the Heights. At ~6.7%, payment-constrained demand is the working assumption until the rate regime changes. The recent split — sale price still near $400k, $/sqft down 7.3% — is consistent with buyers stretching on total price while sellers concede on a per-foot basis, especially on larger new-build product.

05. Income, businesses, and migration

Harris County SAIPE median household income rose from $61,906 in 2020 to $74,682 in 2024. Private establishments have expanded over the same long cycle, from roughly 95,000 around 2010 to about 121,500 in the latest composite. Both series describe purchasing power and local economic density. They are county-level, so they overstate how tightly they map onto a few 77080 blocks.

Harris County resident population is still growing (on the order of 5.05 million in recent estimates). Net domestic migration has been weaker — in some years negative — while international inflows and natural increase have kept the county expanding. That is a metro absorption story. It does not isolate in-migration to 77080. For this ZIP, the binding constraint is product mix and school quality, not lot scarcity.

06. Schools

Public quality is mixed-to-weak, and that is the difference versus the Heights briefing. Cedar Brook Elementary (~7.0, TEA B / 88, GreatSchools 7/10) is the local public anchor. Spring Branch Elementary (~6.0) is solid. Spring Woods High (~3.5, GreatSchools 3–4, Niche B−) is the better of the two zoned high schools but is not a family-premium print. Northbrook High (~2.0, GreatSchools 2/10, Niche C−) is a drag on the buyer pool that will pay for location.

This is Spring Branch ISD, not HISD — and it is the western side of SBISD, not the Memorial High / Stratford side. High-rated private options within a drive — mapped here to ~9.0 on a 0–10 scale — exist, but they are not the zoned default the way a strong elementary is in the Heights. School ratings here are a snapshot, not a time series. They explain willingness to pay more than they explain year-to-year $/sqft moves, and they can change with TEA accountability cycles.

How the pieces fit

The working model is straightforward, and it is not the Heights model. MSA jobs and county income set the bid. 77080 inventory is closer to balanced, so that bid does not convert into $/sqft the way lot scarcity does inside the loop. Mortgage rates time the cycle, and this ZIP feels them more. Schools segment the buyer pool — here, they narrow it. County migration is background noise unless it changes the job or income path. None of these is a sufficient statistic on its own, and past appreciation is not a forecast. Rate, inventory, and employment regimes can reverse.
